# RULE0 -- Checkpoint 0, Rule 0 items 1 to 5

Per office/QUESTION_RULES.md (Rule 0, with item 5, the decision test, added
in the WEBSITE project; the site repo's office/ copy is updated in a later PR)
and office/CHECKPOINTS.md (Checkpoint 0). Rewritten 6 October 2026 around the
reframe that came from the reader tests (office/TITLES.md). Written before any
HDB price was opened. There are no confidence numbers here; Jacob sets those
alone at the seal.

1. **What people argue about.** Million-dollar HDB flats and $100,000 COEs
   are both constant headlines, argued about separately. Few people link
   them.
   - Million-dollar flats (Parliament): HDB resale flats sold for $1 million
     or more rose "from 46 flats in 2017 to 259 flats in 2021" (MND, 4 Jul
     2022). "In 2025, about 6% of resale flats transacted above $1 million"
     (MND, 7 Apr 2026). Files: raw/hansard/.
   - $100,000 COEs (SingStat M651121, data source LTA): Category B was first
     at or above $100,000 in the first bidding of June 2022, and Category A
     in the second bidding of April 2023. Both have stayed above it since
     April 2024 (B) and July 2025 (A), to the latest bidding in September
     2026 (out/coe_crossings.txt).
2. **The belief, and its rival.**
   - Belief: car prices and flat prices are separate stories. A COE is a
     car-owner's problem.
   - Rival (Huang, Li and Ross, 2018, on private condos; tested here on HDB
     flats): when owning a car costs more, living near town is worth more.
     So part of the near-town premium moves with the COE.
3. **What the reader, and the country, stand to gain or lose.**
   - If the rival holds, part of what a family pays for a flat near town
     tracks how scarce cars are, car or no car.
   - The other side: part of what a family saves by buying further out is
     the same thing.
   - The vehicle quota then reaches every flat buyer, not only drivers.
4. **The extremes, with an everyday analogy.**
   - Thought experiment: COEs so dear that almost nobody drives, so every
     flat's price is set by its train ride to work.
   - Or COEs free, a car in every carpark, so a flat far out costs little
     more in time than one near town.
   - Analogy: when the last bus home stops running, the room nearest the
     office is worth more.
5. **Who would think or act differently (the decision test).**
   - **A buyer** weighing a flat near town against one further out. Part of
     that premium may track car scarcity, which can reverse if quotas
     loosen.
   - **Anyone weighing vehicle-quota policy.** They would have to count a
     side-effect on households that never drive.
   - **The debate on pricing new flats near town** (the Prime Location
     model). It gains an input it does not count now.

**The honest limit, stated up front.** At most, this explains part of the
near-town premium, not million-dollar flats as a whole. Nothing here says
where million-dollar flats are; no saved source says they are near town. The
one location line in the sources is MND's (4 Jul 2022): flats at high
prices "tend to be in very favourable locations or come with larger floor
areas". It names no distance or town and is not used as one.

## World view: a place that prices cars differently

London charges for driving in the centre instead of rationing who may own a
car, as Singapore's COE does. Transport for London's page says the
Congestion Charge "is an GBP 18 daily charge (if paid on the day or in
advance) or GBP 21 (if paid by midnight of the third day after travel)",
payable "if you drive within the Congestion Charge zone 7:00-18:00
Monday-Friday and 12:00-18:00 Sat-Sun and bank holidays". (The page writes
the pound sign; GBP here keeps the file in plain ASCII.)

Source: Transport for London, "Congestion Charge",
https://tfl.gov.uk/modes/driving/congestion-charge, saved by Jacob from a
browser on 5 October 2026: cargradient/raw/tfl_congestion_charge.html, md5
1c017bb804f365fc1dff27b436f1e572. The saved page gives no start date for the
scheme. None is stated here, and none is taken from memory. If the piece
needs one, it comes from a second saved TfL source.
