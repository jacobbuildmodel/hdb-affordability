# RULE0 -- Checkpoint 0, the four one-liners

Per office/QUESTION_RULES.md (Rule 0) and office/CHECKPOINTS.md (Checkpoint 0),
site repo main at 30845be. Written before any data was opened. No confidence
numbers here; Jacob sets those alone, later.

1. **What people already believe or argue.** That a COE is a car-owner's
   problem: if you do not buy a car, its price is none of your business.
2. **The sides.** Belief: expensive COEs only hit drivers. Rival (the
   paper's claim, tested here on HDB data): when owning a car costs more,
   being near town is worth more, so flats near the centre get dearer
   relative to flats further out, and part of the COE lands on the price of
   every flat, car or no car.
3. **What the reader stands to gain or lose.** If the rival holds, a family
   buying in an outer town pays less for its flat partly because a car costs
   so much, and a family near town pays more for the same reason; the 2018
   cut in car growth to zero would have widened that gap.
4. **The extremes, with an everyday analogy.** Thought experiment: COEs so
   dear that almost nobody drives, and every flat's price is set by its
   train ride to work; or COEs free, a car in every carpark, and a far-out
   flat costs little more in time than one near town. Analogy: when the last
   bus home stops running, the room nearest your office is worth more.

## World view: a place that prices cars differently

London charges for driving into the centre (the congestion charge, a daily
fee per day of use) instead of rationing who may own a car, as Singapore's
COE does.
