## T2. Financing contributed nothing

PASS, on the peg and two floor-binding anchor points; still not a full rate history.

HDB's own page, saved as `raw/hdb_interest_rate_page.pdf`, establishes the peg: the concessionary rate is "pegged at 0.10% above the prevailing CPF Ordinary Account (OA) interest rate" and is 2.60 per cent for the two quarters the page lists.

CPF Board's quarterly interest rate notices for 1 Jan to 31 Mar 2016 (`raw/cpf2.pdf`) and 1 Jul to 30 Sep 2026 (`raw/cpf3_q3_2026.pdf`) are primary documents a decade apart. Both state the OA rate held at the 2.5 per cent legislated floor rather than the lower market-computed rate, and both state the HDB concessionary rate at 2.6 per cent as the direct, formula-driven consequence of that floor. cpf2.pdf gives the market-computed rate the floor overrode as 0.21 per cent.

That closes the window at both ends on primary sources: the peg formula never changed, and the floor bound at the start of the window and still bound after its end. No month between the two notices has been checked individually, so this is not a verified monthly history, only two dated confirmations that the mechanism producing 2.6 per cent was unchanged before and after. The verdict is upgraded from PARTIAL to PASS on that basis, not on a downloaded time series.

Revision note, 16 September 2026: the PARTIAL verdict was hard-coded in `06_tests.py` before cpf2.pdf and cpf3_q3_2026.pdf, both already checksummed in raw/, were read against this test. No new data was downloaded to produce this revision.

## T7. The median household and the Enhanced CPF Housing Grant ceiling

The EHG ceiling has been 9,000 dollars a month since September 2019. HDB's own page states it: "The average gross monthly household income (all applicants and occupiers) for the months worked during the 12-month period must not exceed $9,000."

    I(2022) = 8904    I(2023) = 9646    C = 9000
    crossing = 2022 + (9000 - 8904) / (9646 - 8904) = 2022.1

**This is an upper bound on HDB's basis, not a point estimate.** SingStat includes one-twelfth of the annual bonus and HDB's assessment excludes bonuses, so the series used here sits above HDB-assessed income. The true crossing is 2022.1 or later. See THESIS_ADDENDUM.md entry 2.

Sensitivity that vindicates the sealed choice of series: the same calculation on the INCLUDING-employer-CPF series gives 2016.8, a difference of 5.4 years. Using the wrong series would have moved the answer by more than five years.

## T3 and T4. Grants, and who they reached

The grant series runs from 2019, because the Enhanced CPF Housing Grant replaced the Additional and Special CPF Housing Grants on 11 September 2019 and the schedule it replaced is not sourced. The Proximity Housing Grant is omitted by design: it has no income test and is conditional on living near a parent, so modelling it would mean assuming a living arrangement.

**T3.** For the 20th-percentile buyer, grants rose by 40000 dollars between 2019 and 2025 while the constant-quality price rose by 235857. The grant increase covered **17.0 per cent** of the price increase, against a 40 per cent threshold. T3 is OUTSIDE BOTH THRESHOLDS, neither a pass nor a fail. F3, which would have made grants a footnote, needed under 10 per cent and is not triggered either. Grants moved, and moved by too little.

Revision note, 25 September 2026: this paragraph previously said "T3 FAILS", contradicting the Summary table, which gives the sealed verdict OUTSIDE BOTH THRESHOLDS: 17.0 per cent is below the 40 per cent pass line and above the 10 per cent F3 line. The sentence was hard-coded in `06_tests.py`; it now reads the same verdict as the table. No number changed and no new data was downloaded to produce this revision.

**T4a FAILS, and in the opposite direction to the one written down.** The mortgage servicing ratio for the 20th-percentile buyer rose +9.3 points, the median +1.8, the 80th percentile +0.6. The sealed test predicted the low-income buyer's change would be at least 5 points SMALLER than the high-income buyer's. It is 8.8 points LARGER.

Fail condition reached: F4b, heterogeneity without sign divergence.

**T4b FAILS.** The 20th-percentile buyer's ratio is higher at the end than at the start, so the grant increases did not fully absorb the price rise for that buyer. Offset is partial at best.

**Why the sealed prediction was wrong, which is the interesting part.** The loan is the smaller of the loan-to-value cap and the price less grants. In 2019, with the cap at 90 per cent, a large grant bound first and reduced the loan directly. By 2025 the cap had been cut to 75 per cent and it binds instead, so an extra dollar of grant no longer reduces the loan at all. It goes to the deposit. For the 20th-percentile buyer the deposit was already fully covered by grants until 2024, so the extra grant did nothing for the monthly repayment. The grant went up and the thing it was doing stopped.

A caution the article must carry: comparing percentage-point changes across buyers whose levels differ this much is not like for like. The 20th-percentile buyer starts at a much higher ratio, so the same proportional worsening produces a much larger point change. In proportional terms the ordering is the same but the gap is narrower: +24.7 per cent, +9.8 per cent and +5.4 per cent for p20, p50 and p80.
